Free browser-based RC building analysis & design · Indian codes

I'm building a free, browser-based structural analysis and design tool for RC building frames, designed to the Indian codes. No install, no licence, no account — it opens in a browser tab. The aim is the part of commercial building-analysis software that engineers actually use day to day, with the learning curve designed out.

The research is done — workflow, solver mathematics, IS code clauses, validation benchmarks. But there are gaps only someone in practice can close, and some of them are safety-critical. I'm not willing to guess a code coefficient, so I'd rather ask you than ship something I can't stand behind.

Four blocks below. Skip anything you can't answer — partial is genuinely useful. Short answers are completely fine.

Block A

The three that gate everything

If you only answer three questions, make it these. They decide whether the design-check half of the tool can ship at all.

A1

Which of these standards do you have current copies of?

IS 456:2000 · IS 1893 (Part 1):2016 · IS 13920:2016
IS 875 Parts 1, 2, 3, 5 · SP 16 · SP 34

A2

Would you be willing to check the code-design implementation against the actual standards?

Rough sense of the time you could give it — an hour? A day spread over a few weeks? No wrong answer, I just need to plan around it.

A3

Are you a licensed / chartered engineer who signs off drawings?

Only asking because it changes how the liability disclaimer is worded. Nothing else depends on it.

Why A1 and A2 matter so much. Without the real BIS text, the tool can still do full analysis — forces, moments, drift, periods, seismic base shear — because that's mathematics I can verify against textbook closed-form solutions. What it cannot do is claim to be a code compliance check. That half is blocked on the books, and on a review by someone who actually practises.

Block B

How you actually work

I've made modelling assumptions from research. I need to know whether they match reality — the whole architecture rests on B1.

B1most important

On a normal RC building job — do you mesh the slabs, or use a rigid diaphragm and distribute slab load to the beams by tributary area / yield-line (45°)?

The entire design assumes the second approach is standard practice. If that's wrong, I need to know now rather than after building it.

B2

Do you design slabs separately using the IS 456 coefficient tables, rather than taking slab design from the analysis model?

B3

Roughly what share of your jobs involve —

  • Shear walls or a lift / stair core
  • Flat slabs (no beams)
  • Transfer plates / podium floors

B4

Typical job size — how many storeys, how many bays each way, rough plan dimensions?

B5

In practice, OMRF (R = 3) or SMRF (R = 5)?

And if SMRF — is the IS 13920 ductile detailing genuinely enforced on site, or is it more of a drawing-office formality?

B6

Does any authority or client ever specifically demand one over the other?

B7

B8

B9

Block C

Code values I could not verify

Every item below came back either contradicted between sources or completely unconfirmed. Getting any of them wrong makes the output unsafe rather than merely wrong — which is why I'd rather leave them blank than fill them in from a blog post. Only worth attempting with the books to hand.

C1

IS 1893 Table 7 — minimum design base shear as a % of seismic weight, by zone.

I have 0.7 / 1.1 / 1.6 / 2.4 % from secondary sources, but zero sources actually confirmed it. What are the real values?

C2

IS 1893 Cl. 7.7.1 — at what building height does dynamic analysis become mandatory?

I've seen 40 m and 90 m quoted, plus 12 m for some cases. Sources directly contradicted each other, and this decides whether equivalent static is even legal for a given building.

C3

IS 1893 Tables 5 and 6 (2016 edition) — the irregularity thresholds.

Specifically mass irregularity % and vertical geometric irregularity %. One source gave me 200% / 150%, which I believe are the 2002 numbers rather than 2016.

C4

IS 1893 Cl. 7.10 — what does the 2016 edition require for an open ground storey (stilt parking)?

A hard prohibition, a multiplier on forces, or just a warning?

C5most important

IS 13920:2016 Cl. 7.2 — strong-column-weak-beam. Is the factor 1.4?

i.e. sum of column moment capacities ≥ 1.4 × sum of beam moment capacities at every joint?

C6most important

IS 13920:2016 Cl. 7.6 — special confining reinforcement.

The Ash formula coefficients, the length l0 over which it's required, and the maximum spacing. I could not get the IS 13920 text at all.

C7

IS 1893 Table 9 — R values beyond OMRF = 3 and SMRF = 5.

Specifically ductile shear wall with SMRF: 4.0 or 5.0? I got both.

C8

IS 1893 Cl. 6.4.2.1 — soil type boundaries by SPT N value.

Two conflicting sets: N > 30 / 10–30 / ≤ 10 versus N > 50 / 15–50 / < 15.

C9

IS 1893 Table 8 — is there an importance factor I = 1.2 row in the 2016 edition, or only 1.0 and 1.5?

C10

IS 1893 Cl. 7.11.1 — storey drift limit of 0.004h.

Is the drift taken directly from the R-reduced design forces, or multiplied back up by something first?

C11

IS 456 Cl. 39.1 — the strain condition for a column section wholly in compression.

This sets the top of the P-M interaction curve.

C12

IS 875 Part 3 — Table 2 k2 values above the 10 m height row, and Table 25 force coefficients Cf for rectangular buildings.

A decision I've already made — argue with it. Until C5 and C6 are confirmed against the real text, the tool will offer OMRF R = 3 only and block SMRF entirely. Letting someone divide their base shear by 5 when the software can't verify the ductile detailing is enforced seemed like the wrong call — that's a 40% force reduction against a promise the tool never checks, and soft-storey failure is the dominant RC collapse mode here. If you think that's over-cautious, say so.
